# Writing Antipatterns (neurotext markers)

Use as a check when a sentence feels off — not a tick-box list. If a flagged
construction reads natural and precise, keep it; if an allowed one reads clunky,
rewrite it. When you find one of these, don't remove it mechanically — first ask
what role it plays in the paragraph's meaning.

- "Not X, but Y" antithesis used more than a couple of times per text.
- Staccato fragments without a subject ("You rebuild. You tweak. You rebuild again.").
- Stacked rhetorical questions (two or three in a row for fake drama).
- Artificial "without X / with X" contrast.
- Em dashes used to skip a thought; prefer a complete sentence.
- Arrow chains (→) standing in for prose.
- Mirror structures "X does… Y does…".
- Meta-announcements ("Let's look at…", "Here's what you'll see…").
- Negative framing ("you don't need to…") where a positive statement works.
- Transition paragraphs that only announce the next block.
- Marketing tone: exclamation marks, "this will change your life",
  "best/perfect/ultimate/complete guide".
- Empty generalizations ("interesting experience", "useful solution",
  "works better") without specifics.
- Abstract market stats as filler (projections that lead to no reader action).
- Literary metaphors in a technical text.
- Headings with two questions ("What is X and how it works") — split into two.

> This is a short starter list. Extend it as required.
